# /research <company> [role or jobs/<slug>]

1. Inputs: the job folder (if any) for role context; `profile/career-context.md` for what matters to the candidate.
2. Sources (all public; cite links): the company's site and careers page (Browser pane for Akamai/JS-heavy sites such as mckinsey.com, bain.com), recent news via WebSearch (last 6 months: results, layoffs/hiring, AI initiatives, Australian office news), Glassdoor/Levels.fyi/Seek company reviews for interview-process and salary signals, the firm's own published interview guidance (e.g. BCG/McKinsey case-interview pages), engineering blogs/GitHub for tech companies, LinkedIn public pages for team size and titles in Perth/Sydney (read-only; do not compile personal data beyond role/title).
3. Write `jobs/<slug>/research.md` or `reports/research-<company>-<today>.md` (≤ 2 pages):
   - **What they actually do here** — the Australian/Perth practice, clients/sectors (mining/energy for Perth), size, leaders by title.
   - **Why now** — recent moves that explain the opening (new AI division, contract win, expansion).
   - **How they hire** — stages, tests (e.g. McKinsey Solve, BCG casey chatbot, coding screens), typical timeline, what they say they value.
   - **Tech/tools** — from the JD and public sources; map each to the candidate's evidence or gap.
   - **Talking points** — 5 things the candidate can say that show they did the homework; 6 questions to ask.
   - **Red flags / unknowns** — contract vs permanent, travel, security clearance, pay signals.
4. Mark anything unverified as such. Keep it factual — no flattery, no speculation presented as fact.
